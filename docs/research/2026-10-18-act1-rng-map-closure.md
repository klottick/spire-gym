## Zero-elite witness and initialized-seed image

Prior reviewed static/model work establishes a complete six-walk topology and
legal room-bucket ordering which assign zero Elites despite a requested quota of
three. The witness retains the supplied path choices, bucket swaps and assignment
history; it is not an arbitrary valid-looking map. Four sibling nodes remain
unassigned during ordinary assignment and are filled only after the bucket is
exhausted. This disproves a universal guarantee over all legal supplied-choice
sequences. It does **not** prove that the sequence is generated by one original
initialized root seed, or that the result is a successful playable map.

Static initialization tracing narrows the missing proof. In the new-run Exordium
constructor, the relic lists and scene setup precede assignment of
`mapRng = Random(Settings.seed + actNum)`, which occurs immediately before map
generation. Map path choices and later room-bucket swaps consume that same map
stream. Their bounds and draw count depend on the preceding graph and any
conditional repair behavior; thus independently legal choices need not be jointly
reachable from one stream state. The witness requires one trajectory to produce
the full path sequence and then the required bucket order at the advanced cursor.

Map-node construction also makes visual/animation draws through libGDX's
`MathUtils.random` overloads. In the inspected constructor these are separate from
the explicitly passed `mapRng` and do not advance it. This resolves that specific
visual-draw concern for the inspected path, not every possible game-mode caller or
global initialization detail.

No seed-space brute force, frequency search, or unreviewed game execution was used.
No exact preimage proof or symbolic solver establishes that the witness is in or
out of the root-seed image. A proof would need to account for XS seed expansion,
the complete state-dependent bounded-draw sequence and cursor, and the bucket
shuffle. A search miss would not prove unreachability. No retry, forced Elite,
defeat, or native empty-set policy is inferred.

## Three-item mapper and input-measure boundary

The reviewed static result concerns the all-seen boss triple and Dead Adventurer's
three category tokens. Both form an ordered triple, seed `java.util.Random` from a
game `randomLong`, and use the two descending swaps for a three-element shuffle.
For a **uniform 64-bit input** the Java seed fold gives uniform internal 48-bit
seeds. Exact integer analysis, including bounded-draw rejection and the second
swap's dependence on the first generator state, gives these counts over
`2^48 = 281474976710656`:

| Final index order | Numerator |
|---|---:|
| 0,1,2 | 46912496118442 |
| 0,2,1 | 46912496115666 |
| 1,0,2 | 46912496118441 |
| 1,2,0 | 46912496118369 |
| 2,0,1 | 46912496121215 |
| 2,1,0 | 46912496118523 |

These exact mapper counts are unequal and sum to the denominator. In Dead
Adventurer, successful searches remove the front token; later category outcomes
are therefore conditional on the upfront permutation and observed prefix, not
fresh IID category draws.

The mapper is known for the analyzed pinned library behavior; the initialized,
history-conditioned input measure is not. A uniform 64-bit input, uniform full
nonzero 128-bit XS state, and actual initialized XS trajectory are different
measures. The latter depends on seed expansion, cursor advancement, prior bounded
rejections and history selection. No supplied prior/cursor ensemble makes that
pushforward unique here.

### Candidate measure for an explicit decision

A defensible *candidate*, not a selected policy, is to feed each semantically
independent Java-seeded shuffle an independent uniform 64-bit input and preserve
the exact deterministic mapper pushforward. This keeps the finite mapper bias and
the correlation between swaps within one invocation while removing incidental
coupling to unrelated mechanics. It is compatible with RNG-001's decorrelation
goal only if the invocations really are semantically independent. It must not
erase a mechanic's state/history conditioning: for example, the event's
without-replacement prefix remains conditional, and the five relic-pool shuffles
are issued successively from relicRng. Whether those pool inputs should be
decorrelated from one another or retain their joint stream law needs an explicit
fidelity decision.

Choosing ideal uniform permutations instead would discard the finite mapper law;
RNG-001 alone does not justify that. Choosing exact root-seed/cursor-conditioned
pushforwards is also not accomplished by the uniform64 table and can retain
unrelated coupling. Research acceptance selects none of these alternatives.

For a uniform64 input, each larger call still has a finite deterministic pushforward,
but the specialized three-item floor-sum count does not generalize itself. Exact
enumeration of `2^48` internal seeds was not attempted. For repeated calls from a
shared RNG, separate marginal tables would not determine the required joint
conditional law. No frequencies or benchmarks are presented as proof.
